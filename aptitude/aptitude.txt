7. Present Perfect vs Simple Past
This difference is very important for placement exams.
    Simple Past	                |   Present Perfect
--------------------------------|--------------------------
I completed the work yesterday.	|   I have completed the work.
She went to Delhi last week.	|   She has gone to Delhi.
He ate lunch two hours ago.	|   He has eaten lunch.


perfect tense:
______________________

Present perfect tense → Have/Has

Past perfect tense    → Had

Future perfect tense  → Will have



Present Perfect Continuous Tense:
-------------------------------------------------
Positive:
Subject + has/have + been + verb-ing

Subject	               Helping verb	Example
I / We / You / They	have been	They have been studying.
He / She / It	         has been	He has been studying.

We use this tense when an action:
- Started in the past.
- Continued for some time.
- Is still happening now, or has just stopped.

Examples:
- I have been studying for two hours.
- She has been working since morning.
- They have been playing cricket for an hour.
important keywords:
----------------------------
1.since
2.for



Past Perfect Continuous Tense:
----------------------------------------------
1. When do we use it?
We use this tense when an action:
- Started in the past.
- Continued for some time.
- Stopped before another past action or past time.
Examples:
1. I had been studying for two hours before my friend arrived.
2. She had been working there for five years before she resigned.
3. They had been playing cricket before it started raining.

8. Incorrect use of infinitives
Incorrect: I enjoy to play cricket.

Correct: I enjoy playing cricket.

Incorrect: I want going home.

Remember:
- Enjoy + V-ing.
- Want + to + V1.
- Decide + to + V1.
- Avoid + V-ing.
- Finish + V-ing.

Active voice and Passive voice:
-----------------------------------------------
Active voice:
-------------------
	When the subject performs the action, the sentence is in Active Voice.

Structure: Subject + Verb + Object

Passive voice:(here object becomes subject)
--------------------
	When the object receives the action, the sentence is in Passive Voice.

Structure: Object + Helping Verb + V3 + by + Subject


Tense	                         Active Voice	       Passive Voice
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Simple Present	                   V1 / V1+s	        am/is/are + V3
Simple Past	                       V2	        was/were + V3
Simple Future	                   will + V1	        will be + V3
Present Continuous	       am/is/are + V-ing	am/is/are being + V3
Past Continuous	                was/were + V-ing	was/were being + V3
Future Continuous	         will be + V-ing	Generally not used
Present Perfect	                  has/have + V3	        has/have been + V3
Past Perfect	                     had + V3	          had been + V3
Future Perfect	                  will have + V3	will have been + V3
Present Perfect Continuous	has/have been + V-ing	Generally not used
Past Perfect Continuous	          had been + V-ing	Generally not used
Future Perfect Continuous	will have been + V-ing	Generally not used
